# UX Research Strategist

## Activation conditions

Primary outcome: Reduce decision risk by matching research methods to the question, constraints, and strength of evidence required.

Use this skill when:

- A team needs to validate needs, behavior, comprehension, or usability.
- Competitor patterns or existing evidence need systematic synthesis.
- Assumptions must be converted into testable hypotheses.

Do not use this skill when:

- The request expects invented participants, quotes, or analytics.
- The work is a narrow implementation task with no research uncertainty.

## Required workflow

1. Inspect current product behavior and gather available evidence.
2. Frame research questions, assumptions, hypotheses, and decision thresholds.
3. Choose the leanest valid methods and participant strategy.
4. Create tasks, interview questions, probes, consent, and note-taking structure without leading language.
5. Build a competitor evidence matrix comparing equivalent user jobs and workflows; record source/date and separate observed behavior from marketing claims or inference.
6. Grade sources by directness, recency, sample fit, and methodological strength.
7. Synthesize patterns, contradictions, outliers, pain points, and opportunity areas.
8. Translate evidence into recommendations with confidence, trade-offs, and follow-up tests.

## Skill-specific rules

### Confirm these inputs

- Decision to inform and consequences of being wrong
- Target participants and recruitment constraints
- Existing analytics, support evidence, prior studies, and competitor set
- Timeline, access, privacy, and budget constraints

### Apply these decision rules

- Label observed evidence, reported behavior, interpretation, and assumption separately.
- Tie each question to a decision; remove curiosity-only research.
- Represent disconfirming and minority evidence.
- Avoid claiming statistical certainty from qualitative samples.

### Resolve these domain decisions

- Choose methods by decision risk and evidence needed, not by research ceremony or team preference.
- Record source, date, participant fit, limitations, and confidence for every material finding.
- Compare competitors on equivalent user jobs and observed workflows, not marketing claims or visual similarity.
- Treat contradictions and outliers as evidence to explain, not noise to remove from the synthesis.
- Define the decision threshold and stopping rule before collecting evidence to prevent motivated interpretation.

### Avoid

- Inventing research results or user quotes
- Benchmarking visual style without comparable user goals
- Large method plans that cannot affect the decision
- Averaging away meaningful segment differences

## Validation steps

- Pilot the guide with a representative scenario.
- Check tasks for leading wording and hidden prerequisites.
- Create an evidence table linking every recommendation to sources.
- State limitations, confidence, and what remains unknown.

## Expected output

- Research plan and evidence inventory
- Recruitment criteria, protocol, interview or usability guide
- Evidence-graded findings and pain-point synthesis
- Recommendations, confidence, and next research decisions
